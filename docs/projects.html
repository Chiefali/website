Title

A Data Science Workflow for Strengthening Tribal Public Health Surveillance: Development and Evaluation of a Social Determinants of Health Dashboard and Training Framework

Abstract

Abstract A Data Science Workflow for Strengthening Tribal Public Health Surveillance: Development and Evaluation of a Social Determinants of Health Dashboard and Training Framework By Joseph Ali Indigenous public health systems operate within structurally constrained data environments characterized by small population sizes, frequent data suppression, racial misclassification, and fragmentation across Tribal, state, and federal data sources. These limitations produce incomplete, unstable, and often non-interpretable estimates, restricting the ability of Tribal public health analysts to generate timely and actionable insights for decision-making. While existing statistical methods address aspects of data sparsity and bias, these approaches are rarely translated into practice due to constraints in data access, computational infrastructure, and local analytic capacity. In addition to these structural data challenges, current approaches to training and education within Tribal public health systems remain limited in their ability to support methodological understanding of these issues. These limitations are further compounded by misalignment between Tribal and state public health systems, where data are often produced, defined, and disseminated within state or federal frameworks that do not reflect Tribal governance, population definitions, or service contexts. This disconnect can make it difficult for Tribal public health analysts to interpret, reconcile, and apply externally generated data within their own settings. Training is often episodic, externally delivered, and focused on the use of specific tools or platforms rather than on developing a conceptual understanding of data limitations such as small-area instability, measurement error, and cross-source inconsistency. Consequently, there is a disconnect between tool use and methodological understanding, limiting both the interpretation of results and the ability to develop or adapt analytic approaches in ways that reflect the nuances of Tribal data and context. As a result, Tribal public health analysts may be expected to use and interpret complex and imperfect data without the training needed to diagnose bias, assess uncertainty, or evaluate the implications of integrating multiple data sources. Critically, there are few accessible frameworks or tools that enable Tribal public health departments to not only recognize these limitations, but to engage with them in a meaningful analytic way. In response, this study develops and evaluates an applied data science workflow for strengthening Tribal public health surveillance under real-world data constraints. In collaboration with Cherokee Nation Public Health (CNPH), we developed an interactive R Shiny dashboard that harmonizes county-level Social Determinants of Health (SDOH) data from federal (ACS5, USDA), state (OK2Share), and Tribal (CNPH) sources, organizing 20 indicators into six SDOH domains: Economic Instability, Education Attainment, Housing and Transportation Barriers, Health Care Access, Environmental Burden, and Social and Community Context. The dashboard includes five analytic modules supporting spatial mapping, county profiling, cluster analysis, tradeoff assessment, and scenario-based exploration. A structured training and capacity-building framework was embedded directly within the dashboard and evaluated through a mixed-methods focus group session and user feedback survey with five CNPH analysts. The evaluation demonstrated strong overall usability (mean rating 4.33 out of 5.0) and high ratings for map navigation and SDOH domain clarity. A critical finding was the distinction between usability and interpretability: while participants navigated the interface with ease, interpretation of standardized metrics—including z-scores, composite domain scores, and percentile rankings—presented greater difficulty (mean score 3.67), underscoring that accessible tools do not automatically produce analytic understanding. The resulting framework provides a practical and theoretically grounded model for locally governed, data-driven public health decision-making in Tribal settings, demonstrating that effective surveillance infrastructure requires not only data integration and visualization, but also embedded training, interpretability support, and iterative refinement aligned with Indigenous Data Sovereignty principles.

1. Tribal Public Health Surveillance Dashboard

Overview

This project developed an interactive dashboard designed to support public health decision-making in Tribal communities. The goal was to bring together multiple sources of health and social data into one accessible platform.

The dashboard helps users explore patterns in community health, compare counties, and better understand factors that influence health outcomes.

Why It Matters

Public health data for Tribal communities is often fragmented or difficult to interpret. This tool helps make data more accessible and useful for real-world decision-making.

Key Features

  • Interactive maps and visualizations
  • Integration of federal, state, and Tribal data
  • Focus on Social Determinants of Health (SDOH)
  • User-friendly design for non-technical audiences
